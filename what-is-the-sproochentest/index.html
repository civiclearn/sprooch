What Is the Sproochentest? A Complete Guide to the Luxembourgish Language Exam for Citizenship

For anyone seeking Luxembourgish citizenship, the Sproochentest plays a central role. This Luxembourgish language exam is a mandatory step in most nationality applications. While the process of becoming a citizen involves several legal and administrative steps, it is the Sproochentest that demonstrates your ability to understand and actively use the national language.

Why the Sproochentest Exists

Luxembourg is a multilingual country where French, German, and Luxembourgish coexist in everyday life. Citizenship, however, requires proof of integration into Luxembourgish society, and one way to show this is by demonstrating competence in Luxembourgish. The Sproochentest was introduced to ensure that future citizens can follow conversations, participate in community life, and respect the country’s linguistic heritage.

Structure of the Exam

The Sproochentest evaluates two main language skills:

  • Oral comprehension: Candidates listen to recordings in Luxembourgish and answer questions about them. These might include everyday situations such as conversations in shops, public announcements, or short dialogues.

  • Oral expression: Candidates must speak Luxembourgish themselves. Typically, this involves a conversation with examiners on daily topics, such as family, work, leisure, or life in Luxembourg.

Unlike many language exams, there is no written part. The focus is entirely on spoken communication and listening. This reflects the government’s intention: future citizens should be able to function in real-life Luxembourgish interactions.

Levels Required for Citizenship

To pass the Sproochentest and qualify for citizenship, applicants must demonstrate:

  • A2 level in oral comprehension (understanding everyday speech, simple sentences, and common topics).

  • B1 level in oral expression (being able to speak in full sentences, describe experiences, and participate in conversations).

These levels follow the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR). They are not advanced, but they do require steady practice and exposure to Luxembourgish.

Who Must Take the Sproochentest

Most adults applying for Luxembourg nationality through naturalisation are required to pass the Sproochentest. However, there are exceptions:

  • Long-term residents who completed schooling in Luxembourg may be exempt.

  • Elderly applicants can sometimes benefit from reduced requirements.

  • Children applying alongside parents are usually not tested.

Still, for the majority of applicants, especially those who arrived in Luxembourg as adults, the exam is unavoidable.

How to Prepare Effectively

Candidates often underestimate the oral expression part. Understanding Luxembourgish is one step, but speaking it confidently is another. Preparation strategies include:

  • Listening daily: Luxembourgish radio, podcasts, and television help you get used to the rhythm of the language.

  • Speaking practice: Language tandems, conversation groups, or private tutors are invaluable.

  • Exam simulations: Practicing typical Sproochentest tasks reduces anxiety on exam day.

Some candidates also keep a vocabulary notebook of common expressions used in everyday conversations, especially around shopping, travel, and family life.

Why the Exam Matters Beyond Citizenship

Even after obtaining nationality, Luxembourgish will remain part of daily life. Many government offices and local communities use Luxembourgish as the default spoken language. By preparing for the Sproochentest, candidates are not only moving closer to Luxembourg citizenship but also gaining the ability to participate more fully in their new society.
